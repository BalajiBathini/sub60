How to do each key workout

Follow the warm-up, main set, recovery, and cool-down. Do not skip the easy portions.

⚡ Intervals · speed

Start with 10–15 minutes easy jogging, then dynamic leg swings, ankle circles, and 2 short relaxed accelerations.

  • Nov 3: 6 × 400 m at 5:45–5:55/km; 200 m easy jog/walk between reps.
  • Nov 10: 5 × 800 m at about 5:55–6:05/km; 2–3 min easy recovery.
  • Nov 17: 4 × 1 km at 5:50–6:00/km; 2–3 min easy recovery.
  • Nov 24: 3 × 1 km at 6:00/km, relaxed and controlled.

Finish with 1–1.5 km easy. If form or pace falls apart, slow down or stop the set.

🎯 Tempo · pace control

  • Nov 12: 1.5 km easy + 3 km at 6:15–6:35/km + 1.5–2 km easy.
  • Nov 19: 1.5 km easy + 4 km at 6:15–6:25/km + 1.5 km easy.

Tempo should feel “comfortably hard”—you can say a short phrase, but not chat freely. If this pace feels too hard, use 6:30–6:45/km.

🌿 Easy / recovery runs

Run at approximately 7:15–8:00/km, or slower if needed. You should be able to speak in full sentences. The purpose is aerobic fitness and recovery—not proving speed.

Strides are short, smooth accelerations, not sprints: run 15–20 seconds briskly, then walk/jog 60–90 seconds.

🛣️ Long runs

  • Nov 8: 10 km easy.
  • Nov 22: 10–11 km easy.

You have already run 14 km, so there is no need to increase distance aggressively. Keep the long run comfortable and finish feeling you could continue.

Bodyweight strength plan

Two short sessions most weeks. Keep 2–3 reps “in reserve”; avoid training to failure. You do not need pull-ups for this 10K.

Strength A · 25–30 min

  • Bodyweight squat — 3 × 12–15
  • Incline push-up — 3 × 8–10
  • Reverse lunge — 3 × 8–10 each leg
  • Glute bridge — 3 × 15
  • Calf raise — 3 × 20
  • Plank — 3 × 30–45 sec

Rest 45–75 sec between sets. Use a stable surface for incline push-ups.

Strength B · 20–30 min

  • Push-ups — 5 × 5 (incline/knee variation if needed)
  • Squat — 3 × 15
  • Walking lunge — 2 × 10 each leg
  • Single-leg glute bridge — 2 × 10 each leg
  • Bird dog — 3 × 10 each side
  • Wall sit — 3 × 30–40 sec

Reduce reps if technique deteriorates. Keep the final week light; no heavy leg strength after Nov 20.

Push-up progression: Start with clean sets of 5. If you cannot maintain a straight body line, elevate your hands on a sturdy bench/table. Build gradually—do not chase a max test during race preparation.

Daily habits & recovery

Before a run

  • Easy runs: water and a light snack if hungry.
  • Quality sessions: familiar carbohydrate snack 30–90 minutes before if useful.
  • Warm up 10–15 minutes before intervals/tempo.
  • Wear familiar running shoes and avoid new gear on race day.

After a run

  • Walk/jog cool-down and gentle mobility.
  • Eat a meal with carbohydrates and protein.
  • Hydrate normally; more in heat/sweat, without forcing excessive water.
  • Aim for 7–9 hours sleep.
  • Do not aggressively diet during this short race block.

Nutrition: include protein in meals and enough carbohydrates to fuel training. Individual needs vary; this is general fitness guidance, not medical advice.

Race-week checklist

Listen to your body.

Stop running and seek medical help for chest pain, faintness, unusual shortness of breath, or severe symptoms. For sharp or worsening pain, do not push through it. If fatigue accumulates, skip an optional run rather than making up missed training. Consistency includes recovery.
